# Making a fragment stageable

You are given one item's span — a stretch of text cut finely enough that it may not stand on its
own — and the people its own references resolve to. Rewrite it as a single sentence something
could stage without reading the document.

> Span: `" as he ran for the door."`
> People: James
> Rewrite: `"James ran for the door."`

## The one rule

**You may restate the span. You may not add to it.**

Exactly three sources are allowed in your rewrite:

1. Words the span itself uses — in any inflection. `ran` may become `runs`.
2. The names of the people listed for you, and only those.
3. Function words: *the, a, was, is, and, of, to, his, her, then, not,* punctuation, `'s`.

Anything else is an invention and the rewrite is thrown away. Replacing "he" with "James" is
right, because James is one of the people the span's own references resolve. Mentioning a door the
span never mentions is wrong. Adding *why* he ran is wrong. Adding an adjective is wrong.

## Keep it to one sentence

The span is one item. Two sentences means you described more than you were given.

## If the span already stands alone

Return it as a well-formed sentence with nothing added. You are never asked to improve prose —
only to make a fragment independently stageable.
