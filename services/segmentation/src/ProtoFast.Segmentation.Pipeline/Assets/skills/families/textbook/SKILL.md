# Textbooks

Expository prose over apparatus, punctuated by direct address to the reader.

- **The majority is `Expounded`** — a voice teaching, with no place and no time. That is correct,
  not a failure to find a setting.
- **The islands are what a renderer wants.** A worked example ("a train leaves Chicago at 3pm")
  builds a miniature world and is `Enacted`; a historical vignette is `Narrated`. These are short
  and they are the only spans with a world to build — do not swallow them.
- **"In this chapter you will…" is `Addressed`**, not `Expounded`.
- **Definitions, theorems, tables and figures are `Exhibited` scenes of their own.**
- **A problem set is one scene**, not thirty. The subject is "the exercises"; the little worlds
  inside individual problems are a rendering decision below the scene.
- **Worked-example names are disposable.** The Alice of exercise 4.2 is not the Alice of exercise
  9.1 — they are scene-local.
- Front matter includes the preface and table of contents; a bibliography is back matter.
