# Novels

Prose fiction: dialogue-heavy, with settings that are usually implied rather than stated.

- **Scene breaks are often typographic** — a blank line, a centred asterisk trio, a small
  ornament — rather than headed. Treat one as a marked boundary.
- **Chapters interrupt scenes constantly.** A scene that continues across a chapter break is the
  ordinary case, not an error.
- **Settings are stated once and then assumed.** A scene with no place claim inside a chapter
  whose previous scene named one is usually still there; a chapter opening usually restates.
- **Dialogue attribution is often implicit.** Two speakers alternating without tags is normal, and
  the cast does not change because the speaker did.
- **Epigraphs at chapter openings are part of the work**, not front matter.
- Front matter is a title page, copyright and dedication; back matter is an about-the-author page
  and advertisements.
